## Abstract

This paper presents a comprehensive economic model of household resource distribution in cat-dominated environments. We demonstrate that cats operate under a unique economic system where all resources flow unidirectionally toward feline satisfaction, challenging traditional supply-demand theories. Our findings suggest that cats have achieved post-scarcity economics through strategic manipulation of human labor.

## Introduction

Traditional economics assumes rational actors making optimal choices. However, in households containing cats, we observe a parallel economy where one species (Felis catus) has successfully externalized all labor costs to another species (Homo sapiens) while maximizing personal utility through strategic napping.

## Theoretical Framework

### The Meow-Keynesian Model

We propose that cat economies operate on three fundamental principles:

1. **Infinite Demand, Zero Production**: Cats maintain constant demand for food, attention, and box-sitting opportunities while contributing nothing to GDP
2. **The Nap Multiplier Effect**: Each hour of cat sleep generates 0.3 hours of human productivity guilt
3. **Cuteness as Currency**: Cats have monopolized the cuteness market, creating artificial scarcity through selective affection distribution

## Methodology

We conducted a 2-year longitudinal study of 300 households, measuring:
- Food expenditure per cat vs. per human
- Square footage occupied by cat beds, towers, and random boxes
- Human productivity loss due to "cat on keyboard" events
- Emotional manipulation frequency (purrs per hour)

We observed random bursts of economic activity (running at 3 AM) that serve no productive purpose. This challenges the efficient market hypothesis and suggests cats operate under "chaos theory economics."
